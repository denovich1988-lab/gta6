    Does GTA 6 Have Difficulty Settings? Every Option That Helps

    GTA 6 difficulty settings explained: there is no easy or hard mode, but Accessible Combat, Reduced Vehicle Damage and targeting options make it easier.

    Status
    Confirmed
    Updated
    November 21, 2026
    Read
    7 min
    By
    GTA6Box team

    Quick answer

    • GTA 6 has no difficulty selector: there is no easy, normal or hard mode to choose.
    • Two toggles under Settings > Gameplay > Accessibility make the game much easier: Accessible Combat and Reduced Vehicle Damage.
    • Targeting mode, Aim Assist Strength, Shooting Assist and Simplified Mini Games let you tune the rest, and all of them can be changed at any time.

    GTA 6 difficulty settings do not include an easy, normal or hard mode: the game has one difficulty, and you adjust it with individual options instead. Two of those options, Accessible Combat and Reduced Vehicle Damage, are easy mode in all but name. Others change how much the game helps you aim, how forgiving button prompts are and how mini-games play. This page lists every option that affects difficulty, where to find it and what to set if you are stuck.

    Menu names come from Rockstar's own support page and from a full listing of the options screens published at launch; effects are stated only where two sources agree or where we name the source.

    GTA 6 difficulty options at a glance

    Option Where Values Effect on difficulty
    Accessible Combat Gameplay > Accessibility Off / On Large: enemies deal less damage
    Reduced Vehicle Damage Gameplay > Accessibility Off / On Large: vehicles are much harder to wreck
    Targeting Gameplay > Combat Lock-On / Partial / Free Aim Large: how much the game aims for you
    Aim Assist Strength Gameplay > Combat Slider Medium
    Shooting Assist Gameplay > Combat Off / On Medium
    Drive-By Assist Gameplay > Combat Off / On Medium, in vehicles
    Simplified Mini Games Gameplay > Accessibility Off / On Small: activities only
    Tap Assist and Hold Assist Gameplay > Accessibility Several levels Small: button prompts
    Auto Drive Gameplay > Accessibility Restricted / Unrestricted Small: driving

    There is no difficulty selector

    VGC and BGR both state it plainly: you cannot pick a difficulty level in GTA 6. Rockstar's support article on settings lists the menu categories (Control Scheme, Controller, Gameplay, Display, UI, Camera, Audio and Account) and none of them is a difficulty menu.

    Why it matters: there is nothing to choose at the start of a new game and nothing you can get wrong. Everything on this page can be changed later from the pause menu.

    Where the settings are

    1. Pause the game.
    2. Open Settings.
    3. Use the shoulder buttons to move to the Gameplay tab.
    4. Gameplay has three groups: Combat, Movement and Accessibility.

    Rockstar's support page adds that Restore Default and Restore All buttons sit at the bottom of the screen if you want to undo your changes.

    Tip: when you change one of the options that affect balance, the game shows a warning that it may significantly change the experience. VGC notes that if you leave without saving, the old values come back, so confirm the change.

    Accessible Combat: the main easy-mode switch

    Accessible Combat is under Gameplay > Accessibility and is off by default. VGC describes it as reducing the damage enemies do to Jason and Lucia, and names it as one of the two toggles that make up an easy mode. The options listing at Playday.One labels it as making the game far easier.

    Why it matters: later missions throw more enemies at you. If a shootout such as the airstrip fight in Slam Dunk keeps ending badly, this is the first thing to switch on.

    Reduced Vehicle Damage

    The second easy-mode toggle sits in the same group. According to VGC, it makes it much harder to disable a vehicle completely by crashing, although several high-speed crashes will still do it. BGR describes it loosely as turning vehicle damage off; the option's actual name, and VGC's description, show that it reduces damage instead of removing it.

    Why it matters: many missions fail or drag when your car dies in a chase. With this on, mistakes at speed cost less. It helps most in escapes from a wanted level.

    Targeting: Lock-On, Partial or Free Aim

    Under Gameplay > Combat, Targeting has three modes:

    Mode What to expect
    Lock-On The most help: aiming snaps to a target
    Partial A middle setting
    Free Aim No lock; you aim entirely yourself

    The names are from the options listing; VGC confirms that lock-on can be switched on to make shooting easier.

    Tip: if you are coming from GTA 5's default aiming, start on Lock-On. Move to Partial once fights feel routine. Free Aim suits players who also play shooters.

    Aim Assist Strength, Shooting Assist and Quick Draw

    Three more Combat options fine-tune gunfights:

    • Aim Assist Strength. A slider. Playday.One's listing shows it running from 0 to 4 with 4 as the default; BGR describes a 0 to 5 scale. Either way, the highest value is the most help and is where the game starts.
    • Shooting Assist. Off or On.
    • Drive-By Assist. Off or On; it applies when shooting from vehicles.
    • Quick Draw. Off or On; BGR describes it as prioritising a fast draw.

    Why it matters: the default settings already lean towards assistance. If combat feels too easy, lowering Aim Assist Strength is the gentlest way to add challenge without touching anything else.

    Simplified Mini Games and button assists

    The Accessibility group also covers everything that is not combat:

    Option Values Use it if
    Simplified Mini Games Off / On Activities such as gym sessions are a barrier
    Tap Assist Off, 2x, 3x, 4x, Hold Rapid button tapping is hard for you
    Hold Assist Off up to 4x Long holds are uncomfortable
    Hold Delay 0% to 250% Prompts trigger too fast or too slowly
    Aiming, Interact, Nitrous Hold / Press You prefer toggles to held buttons
    Fishing Rotate / Hold / Pull The default fishing input is awkward
    Auto Drive Restricted / Unrestricted You want the car to drive itself more often
    Auto Equip Helmet Off / On You ride bikes a lot

    BGR mentions that mini-game difficulty can be adjusted, with gym sessions as its example; in the menu that is the Simplified Mini Games switch.

    Tip: workouts, fishing and similar activities feed challenges and cheat unlocks. If they are what is stopping you, turn on Simplified Mini Games before you give up on GTA 6 challenges.

    Options that help without changing balance

    Some settings make the game easier to read without making enemies weaker:

    • Projectile Arc shows where a thrown object will land.
    • Map, minimap and in-world marker size can be set to Large.
    • GPS Voice is off by default and can be set to tones or speech.
    • Reticle Shape can be Complex, Simple, Dot or None.
    • High Contrast Shaders and colour adjustments are available under Display.
    • Camera Shake and cinematic crash and kill cameras can be reduced.
    • Focus has its own input setting. By default Focus Mode is entered by pressing L3 and R3 together; VGC suggests it for spotting mission items you cannot find.

    Why it matters: a lot of "difficulty" in an open-world game is information. Bigger markers and a visible throwing arc fix problems that no damage setting will. More basics are in how to play GTA 6.

    What is not confirmed

    • Trophies and achievements. No source we checked says whether Accessible Combat or Reduced Vehicle Damage affects trophies. Cheats do disable them; these are settings, not cheats, but that is not proof.
    • A mission skip. Earlier Rockstar games offered a skip after repeated failures. None of the launch guides mentions one in GTA 6.
    • The exact Aim Assist range. Two outlets report different scales, as noted above.
    • An "enemy encounters" setting. BGR refers to adjusting enemy encounters; no option with that name appears in the full listing.
    • A harder mode. Nothing in the menus raises enemy damage or removes checkpoints.

    Recommended setups

    1. Story only, minimum stress. Accessible Combat on, Reduced Vehicle Damage on, Targeting on Lock-On, Simplified Mini Games on.
    2. Default with a safety net. Leave everything as it is; switch on Accessible Combat only for a mission you have failed three times, then switch it off again.
    3. Stuck on driving, fine at shooting. Reduced Vehicle Damage on, everything else default.
    4. Want more challenge. Targeting on Free Aim, Aim Assist Strength down, Shooting Assist off, minimap set to Dynamic or Off.
    5. Before any change. Make a manual save, so you can compare. See how to save in GTA 6.

    What this means for you

    If you were looking for an easy mode, you have one: two switches under Accessibility, available from the first minute and reversible whenever you like. If you were looking for a hard mode, you have to build it yourself by taking assists away. And if you only want the game to be clearer, the UI and navigation options do more than the combat ones. Since missions cannot be replayed (see can you replay missions in GTA 6), there is no score to protect by leaving the assists off.

    Playing in first person changes how aiming feels as well; see GTA 6 first-person mode.

    Frequently asked questions

    Does GTA 6 have difficulty settings?

    Not as a single choice. There is no easy, normal or hard mode, but separate options such as Accessible Combat, Reduced Vehicle Damage, Targeting and Aim Assist Strength change how demanding the game is.

    How do you turn on easy mode in GTA 6?

    Pause, open Settings, go to the Gameplay tab and find the Accessibility group. Switch on Accessible Combat and Reduced Vehicle Damage, then save the change when the warning appears.

    Can you change the difficulty during the story in GTA 6?

    Yes. VGC notes that the easy-mode toggles can be switched on or off at any time.

    Does GTA 6 have aim assist?

    Yes. Targeting has three modes, Lock-On, Partial and Free Aim, with a separate Aim Assist Strength slider and a Shooting Assist toggle.
