    GTA 6 Ending Explained: How Many Endings and What Decides Yours

    The GTA 6 ending explained with light spoilers: how many endings guides count, the final choice, and how profile, relationship and savings decide yours.

    Status
    Partly confirmed
    Updated

    Quick answer

    • GTA 6 has several endings: GTABase and Detonated count five, Beebom counts four, and Rockstar has not published a list.
    • The last mission, The End of the Road, ends with a choice between Swim for It and No Surrender; what follows depends on your save.
    • Three things decide the outcome: criminal profile, relationship level and the money in Family Savings. Guides disagree on the exact amounts.

    The GTA 6 ending is not a single scene: the game has at least four endings, and the one you see depends on a last choice plus three things you have been building all game, namely your criminal profile, your relationship level and your Family Savings. This page explains how the ending is decided, what the guides agree on, where they contradict each other, and what to do before the final chapter if you want the best outcome. It contains light spoilers: we describe who lives, dies or goes to prison, but not the story reasons.

    How we checked: every fact below appears in at least two of three independent ending guides (GTABase, Beebom and Detonated). Where only one guide says something, or where they disagree, we say so. Rockstar has not published a list of endings.

    1. GTA 6 has four or five endings, depending on who is counting

    GTABase lists five endings and ranks them from worst to best. Detonated also lists five. Beebom lists four and calls them two good and two bad. The lists overlap but are not identical: each site has at least one ending the others do not describe.

    Beebom also reports a Rockstar comment that the story has "a lot more than two" conclusions and that the studio does not think of them as good or bad. We have not seen the original statement, so treat that as reported.

    Why it matters: if you read "five endings" on one site and "four" on another, neither is necessarily wrong. They are grouping variations differently. The three outcomes below are the solid part.

    2. The three outcomes every guide agrees on

    Stripped of names, all three guides describe the same three results:

    Outcome What happens How guides label it
    Both die Jason and Lucia drive at the roadblock and are killed Bad; "Bonnie and Clyde" on all three sites
    One escapes, one is jailed Lucia gets away and Jason goes to prison Good or bittersweet
    Both go free Jason is released and the family leaves together Best

    GTABase and Beebom describe a fourth result as well: the pair try to give up, it goes wrong, and they are shot. Detonated has a similar ending in the water. That is three sources for a second bad ending, with different details in each.

    Tip: if all you want is to avoid the two worst endings, the next two sections are the ones that matter.

    3. The final choice: Swim for It or No Surrender

    The last mission is The End of the Road. It closes Chapter 5, the shortest of the game's five chapters. GTABase and Beebom both describe a roadblock on a bridge, where the game offers two options:

    Choice Result
    No Surrender The both-die ending. GTABase says this happens whatever your save looks like
    Swim for It One of the other endings, picked by the state of your save

    GTABase gives the prompts as Triangle (Y on Xbox) for Swim for It and R2 (RT) for No Surrender. Only one guide lists the buttons, so read the screen before you press anything.

    Why it matters: No Surrender is the one option that closes off every better ending. Beebom's wording leaves a little room for survival on that path, but GTABase is flat about it, and no guide describes anyone living through it.

    4. Criminal profile: the first thing the game checks

    All three guides tie the ending to the criminal profile, the rating the game gives each lead for how violently they behave. GTABase lists its tiers as Professional, Hardened, Violent, Psycho and Irredeemable; Beebom uses the same names for the tiers it mentions.

    • Clean profile (Professional or Hardened): needed for the best ending, according to GTABase and Beebom.
    • Psycho or Irredeemable: leads to a bad ending even if you choose Swim for It, according to both.

    Detonated agrees that a high profile on either character ends in death. Its description of the best ending says the opposite about profiles, which contradicts the rest of its own page; we read that as an error.

    Tip: our walkthroughs flag the moments that move the profile. Shooting tires and legs instead of drivers in First Impressions, or steering the safe away from cyclists in Safe Word, are typical. Small choices like these add up over a very long story; see how long GTA 6 is.

    5. Relationship level: keep it at the top

    GTABase requires the top relationship level, Hot Together, for both of its good endings. Beebom says the relationship must be maxed for the best one. Detonated asks for a high relationship rating.

    The relationship meter rises with everyday things, such as time together, answering messages and pulling off jobs as a pair, and falls with neglect. How it works is covered in GTA 6 romance.

    Why it matters: this is the requirement you cannot fix with money or a single replay of the last mission. It is built over the whole story.

    6. Family Savings: $2 million or $3 million

    Family Savings is the shared account you open in Buck Me early in Chapter 1. All three guides say a large balance there is needed for the best ending:

    Guide Amount for the best ending
    GTABase $2,000,000
    Detonated $2,000,000
    Beebom At least $3,000,000

    Two guides against one favours $2,000,000, and GTABase and Beebom both mention a payment of $2.5 million in the best ending itself. GTABase adds that with a clean profile and top relationship but less than $2,000,000, you get the ending where one lead goes to prison.

    Tip: if you can, bank $3,000,000 before Chapter 5. That satisfies every guide. Robberies become a steady earner from Credit Check onward; more methods are in how to make money in GTA 6.

    7. Which ending is the "good" one?

    GTABase and Beebom both name the same best ending: Lucia escapes, Jason is arrested, and she later gets him out, so the family ends up together. Beebom and Detonated both say they move to Liberty City. Detonated does not rank its endings.

    The middle ending, in which Lucia raises their son while Jason is in prison, is called good by GTABase and bittersweet by Beebom.

    Why it matters: nothing here is "canon" in any official sense. Rockstar has not named a true ending.

    8. What happens after the credits

    GTABase and Beebom agree on what opens up once the story is over:

    • Free roam continues, with unfinished side missions, robberies and collectibles still available.
    • The Cheats section appears with 61 cheats. Some work at once and the rest need their own unlock tasks; see GTA 6 cheats.
    • A 100% checklist appears under Stats in the Progress menu; see GTA 6 100% completion.

    All three guides mention a short scene after the credits involving Cal, Jason's friend from the Keys. They describe it differently, so we will leave the details to the game.

    9. You can replay the finale, with limits

    GTA 6 has no mission replay; that is covered in can you replay missions in GTA 6. The ending is the reported exception. GTABase and Beebom both say that after the credits you are put back just before The End of the Road and can play it again with a different choice.

    They differ on how much you can change. GTABase says you can top up your savings and relationship before trying again. Beebom says changing the profile or relationship needs an older save.

    Tip: do not rely on either. Make a manual save in its own slot at the start of Chapter 5; how to save in GTA 6 explains the slots.

    What is not confirmed

    • The exact number of endings. Four or five, with no official list.
    • A deal ending before the final mission. GTABase alone describes an early bad ending triggered by accepting an offer at the end of Protective Custody, the mission before the finale. No other guide mentions it.
    • An ending where Jason escapes and Lucia is jailed. Only Detonated lists it.
    • A "save the cop" requirement for the best ending. Only Detonated mentions it, without saying where it happens.
    • The savings threshold. $2,000,000 on two sites, $3,000,000 on one.
    • Whether No Surrender can ever be survived. GTABase says no; Beebom's wording is looser.
    • Trophies tied to the ending. GTABase names two hidden trophies for the final mission and its choices. We will add them to the trophy guide when a second list matches.

    How to set up the best ending: a checklist

    Do these before you start Chapter 5. GTABase says the finale begins with a point-of-no-return warning.

    1. Save in a separate slot before the last chapter.
    2. Check both criminal profiles. Aim for Professional or Hardened on Jason and on Lucia.
    3. Raise the relationship to its top level, Hot Together.
    4. Put $3,000,000 in Family Savings if you can, and at least $2,000,000 if not. The guides count the balance of that account, so deposit your cash.
    5. At the bridge, choose Swim for It.
    6. If you get an ending you do not want, reload the Chapter 5 save and fix what was missing.

    What this means for you

    The ending is a report card on the whole playthrough. One button press at the bridge matters, but only as a veto: No Surrender gives the worst result, and Swim for It hands the decision back to how you played. If you kept the violence down, looked after the relationship and saved money, the game rewards you. If you are close to the finale with a Psycho profile and an empty savings account, expect a bad ending and plan a second run at it from an earlier save.

    Frequently asked questions

    How many endings does GTA 6 have?

    Guides count four or five. GTABase and Detonated list five, Beebom lists four, and their lists do not match one for one. All three agree on three outcomes: both leads die, one escapes while the other goes to prison, and both go free.

    How do you get the best GTA 6 ending?

    Reach the finale with a clean criminal profile, a maxed relationship and a large balance in Family Savings, then choose Swim for It. GTABase and Detonated say $2,000,000 in savings; Beebom says at least $3,000,000.

    Can you replay the GTA 6 ending?

    GTABase and Beebom both say that after the credits you are placed back before The End of the Road and can play it again. Beebom adds that changing your profile or relationship needs an older save.

    What unlocks after the GTA 6 ending?

    Free roam continues, the Cheats section opens with 61 cheats, and a 100% completion checklist appears under Stats in the Progress menu.
