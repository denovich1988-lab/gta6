    Does GTA 6 Run at 60 FPS? Graphics and Performance

    GTA 6 targets 30 FPS on PS5, PS5 Pro and Xbox Series X|S. There is no 60 FPS mode at launch. What the PS5 Pro adds and what this means for you.

    Status
    Confirmed
    Updated

    Quick answer

    • Rockstar confirmed on November 10, 2026 that GTA 6 targets 30 FPS on every console.
    • There is no 60 FPS or performance mode at launch, including on PS5 Pro.
    • The PS5 Pro gets ray-traced shadows and PSSR upscaling instead.

    No, GTA 6 does not run at 60 FPS: it targets 30 frames per second on every console, including the PS5 Pro, and there is no performance mode at launch. Rockstar confirmed the target on November 10, 2026, in the same Newswire post that announced pre-loading. This page sets out what each console gets, why Rockstar chose 30, what critics made of it, which settings make it feel smoother, and where 60 FPS could realistically come from.

    Frame-rate targets and PS5 Pro features are as Rockstar announced them, reported by Notebookcheck and other outlets. Anything found in pre-load files is labelled as unofficial.

    GTA 6 frame rate at a glance

    Console Frame rate target 60 FPS mode Extras
    PlayStation 5 30 FPS No DualSense haptics, Tempest 3D Audio
    PS5 Pro 30 FPS No Ray-traced shadows, PSSR upscaling
    Xbox Series X 30 FPS No None listed
    Xbox Series S 30 FPS No Lower resolution

    Rockstar has not published an official resolution table for each console.

    What Rockstar confirmed

    The November 10 announcement gave one target for the whole generation: 30 FPS on PS5, PS5 Pro and Xbox Series X|S. According to Notebookcheck's report, Rockstar tied the decision to keeping the density and visual quality of the open world, and did not rule out further graphics modes later. It also did not promise any.

    A target is not a guarantee. Notebookcheck points out that aiming for 30 does not mean the game holds 30 in every scene.

    Why it matters: there is no menu choice to make. You will not find a Performance or Quality toggle on any console, so the question of which mode to use does not arise.

    What the PS5 Pro adds

    The Pro runs at the same 30 FPS target. What it gets instead:

    • Ray-traced shadows, described as exclusive to the Pro.
    • PSSR, Sony's upscaling technology, which the base PS5 does not have.

    Why it matters: the Pro version should look sharper and more grounded, not smoother. If frame rate is your reason for considering a Pro, GTA 6 does not justify it at launch. The full comparison is in best console for GTA 6.

    Base PS5 and Xbox Series X|S

    On the base PS5, Rockstar listed DualSense haptic feedback, Tempest 3D Audio and optimised loading. No Xbox-specific features were announced. Rockstar has not said what resolution or which cutbacks the Xbox Series S version uses; Notebookcheck notes only that the console has far less graphics power and memory than the Series X.

    The official Extended Look, embedded above, was captured on a base PS5, so it is a fair guide to what that console shows.

    Tip: both Xbox consoles and both PlayStations run the same game at the same target. Choose on price, storage and where your friends play; see GTA 6 platforms.

    Why 30 FPS

    Rockstar's stated reason is the world itself: the number of people, vehicles and animals on screen and the detail they are drawn with. A simulation that dense is limited by the processor as much as by the graphics chip, which is why lowering resolution alone would not double the frame rate.

    There is precedent. GTA 5 ran at 30 FPS on the consoles it launched on, and Red Dead Redemption 2 launched at 30 FPS as well. Rockstar's console games have consistently spent their budget on the world first.

    Why it matters: the 30 FPS target is a design decision, not a bug waiting for a patch. Expect it to stay through this console generation unless Rockstar says otherwise.

    What reviewers said about performance

    The frame rate is the most common complaint in reviews of a game that otherwise scored 99 on Metacritic. At least one top-score review listed there names the 30 FPS cap among its few criticisms, and VGC's review mentions minor performance dips while saying they do not spoil the game.

    None of the major outlets lowered a score to below 9 because of it. The round-up is in GTA 6 reviews.

    Why it matters: if you can play other 30 FPS games comfortably, critics suggest you will stop noticing. If you are used to 60 and sensitive to the difference, expect an adjustment period of an hour or two.

    Settings that make 30 FPS feel better

    The game has no frame-rate option, but several camera and display settings change how smooth it feels. These names are from the launch options listing:

    Setting Where Suggestion
    Motion Blur Strength Display > Visual Effects Keep some on; blur hides the gaps between frames
    Camera Shake Camera Lower it if fast driving feels juddery
    Weapon Sway and Camera Bob Camera Lower them for a steadier picture on foot
    Aim Response Curve Controller Try Linear or Precision if aiming feels sluggish
    Stick deadzones Controller Reduce slightly for quicker response

    And on your television:

    1. Turn on Game Mode to cut input delay.
    2. Switch off motion smoothing; it adds lag and artefacts.
    3. If your TV supports VRR, leave it on. It will not raise the frame rate but can help with consistency.

    Tip: change one setting at a time and drive the same stretch of road after each change. More options that affect how the game plays are in GTA 6 difficulty settings.

    Could 60 FPS come later?

    Rockstar has not announced it. Three routes are possible, and none is confirmed:

    • A patch. Rockstar did not rule out additional graphics modes, which is not the same as planning one.
    • A 40 FPS mode. Notebookcheck reports that pre-load metadata contains a reference to 120 Hz output, which led to speculation about a 40 FPS option for 120 Hz screens. The same report says this does not confirm any such mode.
    • A later version. GTA 5 only received a 60 FPS mode when it was re-released on newer consoles years after launch.

    A PC version would be the most likely place for higher frame rates, but it has no date: see GTA 6 on PC and the expected PC requirements.

    What is not confirmed

    • Resolutions. No official native or upscaled figures for any console.
    • Series S cutbacks. Not disclosed.
    • A 40 FPS or 120 Hz mode. Datamined hints only.
    • Measured frame rates. We have not yet seen two independent technical analyses with matching numbers, so we do not quote dips or averages.
    • Any 60 FPS patch. Not announced.

    What to do first

    1. Set your TV to Game Mode and turn off motion smoothing.
    2. Leave motion blur on at first; lower it later if you dislike the look.
    3. Play for an hour before judging. The first minutes after a 60 FPS game are the worst.
    4. If aiming feels heavy, adjust the response curve and deadzones before blaming the frame rate.
    5. Keep the game updated; performance fixes arrive in patches, tracked in GTA 6 updates.

    What this means for you

    GTA 6 is a 30 FPS game on every console you can buy today, and nothing official suggests that will change soon. That makes the choice of console simpler: none of them is the smooth one. Pick the PS5 Pro for image quality, any other for value, and wait for PC only if 60 FPS matters more to you than playing now.

    For what the game looks like in motion, see GTA 6 gameplay.

    Frequently asked questions

    Is GTA 6 60 FPS on PS5?

    No. GTA 6 targets 30 FPS on PS5, and no 60 FPS mode has been announced.

    Does GTA 6 run at 60 FPS on PS5 Pro?

    No. The PS5 Pro targets 30 FPS too, with ray-traced shadows and PSSR upscaling as its extras.

    Does GTA 6 have a performance mode?

    Not at launch. Rockstar has announced one frame rate target for all consoles.
