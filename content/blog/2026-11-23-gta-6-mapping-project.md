**The GTA 6 mapping project is a fan-made map of Leonida, drawn by a volunteer community years before the game came out; it is not an official Rockstar map and never was.** Fans pieced it together from trailers, screenshots and real Florida geography, then redrew it again and again as new footage arrived. This page explains who is behind it, how it was built, what it got right and wrong as far as anyone has shown, and which map you should actually use now that GTA 6 is out.

How we checked this: every claim below comes from Rockstar, from the project's own public posts, or from coverage by PC Gamer, GamesRadar+ and other outlets named in the text. Where only one outlet reports something, we say so. We do not use or link leaked material.

## What the GTA 6 mapping project is

It is a shared drawing. A group of fans set out to work out the shape of GTA 6's world before Rockstar showed it, and to keep one master image up to date as evidence came in. Each release was a numbered draft. Sportskeeda covered version 0.047 as news in its own right, and Gameranx did the same for a later version labelled "YANIS V10", which tells you how closely the wider fan base followed each redraw.

The project produced a flat, top-down map: coastlines, roads, towns and landmarks, with each feature placed where the community's evidence said it belonged. It was never a playable world and it contains no Rockstar assets.

**Why it matters:** for about four years before launch, this was the closest thing to a map that existed. Most "GTA 6 map" images you saw on social media before November 2026 were this project's work or copies of it. If you want what Rockstar itself confirmed, start with [every confirmed region of Leonida](/map/gta-6-map/).

## Who runs it and where it lives

Two places hold the project together.

- **GTAForums.** The work is organised in a thread titled "Mapping Vice City / Map Discussion Thread". A PC Gamer headline described it as a hivemind of speculation, educated guesses and meticulous analysis, with more than 10,500 posts and three years of research behind it. A separate PC Gamer report from June 2025 says the project had been running since September 2022.
- **Discord.** Day-to-day discussion happens on a mapping Discord server, which PC Gamer has cited as a source of the project's news.

The map image itself is credited to a mapper who goes by **DuPz0r**. The fan account **Church of GTA** shared an "updated map" from DuPz0r on X in early October 2022 and credited the Discord group for the work, which fits that timeline. Gameranx credits a later version to a GTAForums user called lxr. We only name people whose handles appear in public posts or in published reports.

**Tip:** many websites now use "GTA 6 mapping project" in their titles. Almost all of them are separate sites writing about the project or running their own interactive maps. The community itself works in the forum thread and the Discord server.

## How fans built a map before the game existed

The method is closer to detective work than to drawing. From how Gameranx, GamesRadar+ and PC Gamer describe the process, it comes down to four habits:

1. **Collect every official frame.** Each trailer and screenshot is broken into single shots.
2. **Find the same landmark twice.** A tower, bridge or stretch of coast seen from two angles lets you estimate where the camera stood and how far apart things are.
3. **Compare with the real Florida.** Leonida is Rockstar's version of the state, so real Miami streets and coastlines are a useful first guess for the layout of [Vice City](/map/gta-6-vice-city/).
4. **Argue about it in public.** A placement goes on the map only after other members have tried to knock it down.

It does not always work. A PC Gamer headline quotes one mapper saying that ten people searched every street in Street View to pin down a location, and that the effort "took weeks and failed". The same headline calls the whole exercise painstaking, exhausting and heroic. Gameranx adds that some mappers use the corner points of buildings to work out the angles between them.

**Why it matters:** the method explains both the project's strengths and its limits. Places that appear in many shots, such as the city's beachfront, can be placed with confidence. Places seen once, from one angle, are guesses.

## The trailers that moved the map forward

Official footage arrived rarely, and each release reshaped the map.

| Official release | Date | What it meant for the mappers |
|---|---|---|
| Trailer 1 | December 2023 | The first real look at Vice City. GamesRadar+ wrote that the community could "finally get to work" on drawing the whole game |
| Trailer 2 and the official website | May 6, 2025 | Named regions and far more scenery. GamesRadar+ reported the community "connecting the dots" a full year before release |
| An Extended Look | August 2026 | Long stretches of in-game footage shortly before launch |

Rockstar's website also named the six regions of the state: Vice City, the Leonida Keys, Grassrivers, [Port Gellhorn](/map/port-gellhorn-gta-6/), Ambrosia and Mount Kalaga National Park. That gave the mappers official names to attach to areas they had already sketched.

**Tip:** if you want to see the footage the map was built from, every official video is listed with dates in [GTA 6 trailers](/news/gta-6-trailers/).

## The rule about leaks

The forum thread carries its rule in its title: "NO LEAK FOOTAGE ALLOWED". That matters because a large amount of early development footage of GTA 6 was stolen and posted online in 2022, and a map built on stolen material would have put the forum at risk.

The picture is less tidy across the wider project. PC Gamer wrote in June 2025 that most of the map was based on information from the 2022 leak, such as the setting. Information is not the same thing as footage, but it does mean the map was never built from official trailers alone. We are not in a position to say which parts of any version rest on which evidence.

**Why it matters:** this is the main reason to treat the fan map as a fan map. Its sources are mixed, and nobody outside the project can audit them. Our own pages use only what Rockstar published or what two independent guides agree on; the official timeline is in [how long GTA 6 was in development](/blog/how-long-was-gta-6-in-development/).

## The biggest correction: losing the panhandle

The clearest example of the project correcting itself is the panhandle. Drafts had included one from the beginning: a north-western arm of land, as real Florida has. In June 2025 PC Gamer reported that people on the mapping Discord expected it to go, with one fan bracing for "the giant list of cuts". On January 6, 2026, Gameranx reported that the newest version of the map had removed it. The reason given was simple: the mappers had found too little evidence for the region and had been guessing, and they said they would draw it back in if later material showed it.

**Why it matters:** a whole region disappearing from a map that many people had treated as settled shows how provisional every version was. If you saved a copy of the fan map before 2026, it shows land that the project itself later decided it could not support.

## How big the fan map said Leonida was

Size was the number everyone wanted, and plenty of sites quoted figures measured from the project's drafts. These were measurements of a drawing, not of the game, and they changed with each version.

Rockstar's own statement is simpler: its developers say Leonida is about twice the size of the GTA 5 map. Rockstar has not published an area in square miles or kilometres.

**Tip:** ignore any exact area figure that cites the mapping project. Our [GTA 6 map size](/map/gta-6-map-size/) page lists only what Rockstar has said and explains why the square-mile numbers online disagree.

## The playable version that Take-Two removed

The fan map inspired other projects. The best known was a mod by a creator known as Dark Space that rebuilt a version of the layout inside GTA 5, so players could drive around an approximation of the new state. He said it was based on a community mapping project, along with leaks and trailer shots. In March 2025 PC Gamer reported that Take-Two, Rockstar's parent company, had made copyright claims against his YouTube videos of the mod, and that he then took the download links down himself. The article quotes his guess that the map "was probably a little too accurate".

That mod was a separate piece of work from the mapping project. The flat community map stayed online.

**Why it matters:** it shows where Take-Two acted. A drawing discussed on a forum was left alone; videos of a playable recreation were not. Anyone waiting for mods of the real game should read [GTA 6 on PC](/pc/gta-6-pc-release-date/), since there is no PC version yet.

## Fan map, official map and interactive maps: the differences

Three different things get called "the GTA 6 map".

| | Mapping project (fan map) | In-game map | Interactive web maps |
|---|---|---|---|
| Made by | Volunteers | Rockstar | Guide sites and fans |
| Based on | Trailers, screenshots, real Florida | The game itself | The released game |
| Available | Before launch | In the game since November 19, 2026 | After launch |
| Shows | Estimated layout and landmarks | Roads, blips, your position, discovered areas | Collectibles, activities and shops as pins |
| Reliability | An estimate, revised many times | Authoritative | Depends on the site |

**Tip:** for finding things, the fan map is the wrong tool. Use the in-game map for navigation and a post-launch guide for item hunting; our [GTA 6 collectibles](/map/gta-6-collectibles/) hub lists every collectible type and its total.

## What is not confirmed

- **How accurate the final draft was.** We have not found a full comparison between the project's last pre-launch version and the released map from an outlet we would rely on. Pre-launch reports that called it close were judging it against other unofficial material, not against the game.
- **What the project is doing now.** We have not seen a statement from the mappers about their plans since launch day.
- **An exact start date.** PC Gamer gives September 2022, and a public update exists from early October 2022, but we cannot give a first day.
- **Member numbers.** Figures quoted around the web are not sourced to the project.
- **Rockstar's view.** Rockstar has not commented on the project in public.

## How to use the maps today

If you are playing now, this is the practical order.

1. **Open the in-game map first.** It is the only complete, correct map of Leonida. Areas fill in as you explore.
2. **Learn the six regions.** Our [region guide](/map/gta-6-map/) explains what each one is and where it sits.
3. **Set up fast travel early.** The RydeMe app saves a lot of driving across a state this size; see [GTA 6 fast travel](/map/gta-6-fast-travel/).
4. **Use post-launch guides for hunting.** Collectibles, [animals](/map/gta-6-animals/) and [easter eggs](/blog/gta-6-easter-eggs/) need positions checked in the released game, not on a pre-launch drawing.
5. **Treat the fan map as history.** Compare an old draft with your own map to see what the community got right. Do not navigate by it.
6. **Stay away from leak downloads.** Files offered as "the leaked map" or a "playable GTA 6 map" are not something Rockstar or the forum community distributes.

## What this means for you

The GTA 6 mapping project was a remarkable piece of fan work: years of patient, public argument that produced a believable map of a game nobody outside Rockstar had played. It was also wrong in places, by its own admission, and it mixed kinds of evidence that outsiders cannot check.

If you are curious about how fans read trailers, it is worth an evening. If you just want to get from Vice City to Mount Kalaga, use the map in the game and the [beginner's guide](/missions/gta-6-beginners-guide/).
