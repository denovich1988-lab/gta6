**The GTA 6 hacker is Arion Kurtaj, who broke into Rockstar Games in September 2022, when he was 17, and posted 90 clips of early development footage online.** He was arrested within days, a jury found in 2023 that he had carried out the hack, and he spent most of the next three years in a secure hospital. The case only closed this week: he pleaded guilty on November 16, 2026, three days before the game launched, and was sentenced on November 23. This page sets out the whole story in order, with dates, and what it did and did not change for the game.

How we checked this: every fact comes from Rockstar and Take-Two's own statements, the Crown Prosecution Service, or court reporting that at least two reputable outlets agree on. We do not describe, host or link to the stolen material.

## What happened in September 2022

Early on Sunday, September 18, 2022, a user on the GTAForums fan site posted 90 video clips from an unfinished build of the next Grand Theft Auto. Court reporting later put the total at close to an hour of footage. It was early development material, well over a year before Rockstar showed the game itself.

According to the Crown Prosecution Service (CPS), the poster used the alias "TeaPotUberHacker", had broken into Rockstar with the intention of blackmailing the company, and threatened to release the game's source code as well. Court reports from 2023 add that he announced himself inside Rockstar's internal Slack workspace and demanded contact before the clips went public.

**Why it matters:** this was a break-in, not a slip by an employee. That distinction is why it ended in a criminal court rather than in an internal investigation, and why it is treated differently from ordinary "leaks" and rumours.

## What Rockstar and Take-Two said

Rockstar answered the next day, September 19, 2022. Its statement, which Take-Two also filed with the US Securities and Exchange Commission, said that an unauthorised third party had illegally accessed and downloaded confidential information, including early development footage for the next Grand Theft Auto. It said live services were not disrupted, that work on the game would continue as planned, and that the studio did not expect a long-term effect on its projects. Rockstar added that it was extremely disappointed to have details of the game shared this way.

Take-Two also moved to have the footage removed. Videos disappeared from YouTube at its request, and takedown notices went to GTAForums and the GTA 6 subreddit, which then banned links to the material.

**Why it matters:** the statement was the first time Rockstar acknowledged any footage of the game at all. Until then it had only said, in February 2022, that development was under way. See [how long GTA 6 was in development](/blog/how-long-was-gta-6-in-development/).

## Who leaked GTA 6

The person responsible was Arion Kurtaj, from Oxfordshire, a member of the Lapsus$ cybercrime group. He was 17 at the time, so UK reporting restrictions kept his name out of the early coverage; he was named in reports of the 2023 verdict, by which time he was 18.

Rockstar was not his only target. The CPS lists attacks on BT and EE in 2021, on the fintech company Revolut, and on Uber, where the attack cost more than $2.8 million. The Uber and Rockstar intrusions both took place in September 2022.

The circumstances were unusual. He was already on bail over earlier hacking allegations and had been moved to a hotel for his own protection. The CPS says he broke his bail conditions there and carried on using a mobile phone, the hotel television and a Fire Stick.

**Why it matters:** the "GTA 6 leaker" was not a Rockstar insider and not a group of developers. The answer to "who leaked GTA 6" is one teenager acting as part of a wider hacking group, which is also why nothing similar followed from inside the studio.

## The arrest

City of London Police arrested a 17-year-old in Oxfordshire on September 22, 2022, four days after the clips appeared, in an investigation supported by the National Crime Agency. Police said he was charged with breaching bail and with computer misuse offences and was kept in custody. At the time the force would not say which incident the arrest related to; the link to Rockstar was confirmed in court later.

**Why it matters:** the speed of the arrest is the reason the threatened follow-up never turned into a long campaign against the studio.

## The 2023 trial and the hospital order

The case reached Southwark Crown Court in the summer of 2023 and lasted seven weeks. Before it began, a medical assessment had found him unfit to plead. That changed what the jury was asked: not whether he was guilty in the usual sense, but whether he had done the acts alleged. In August 2023 the jury found that he had, on all 12 charges. A 17-year-old co-defendant, who was not named, was dealt with in the same trial and later received an 18-month youth rehabilitation order.

In December 2023 the judge made a hospital order with no end date: he would stay in a secure hospital until doctors judged he was no longer a danger. According to reports of the hearing, the court heard that he had autism and that he had said he intended to return to cybercrime. We are not adding anything about his health beyond what was said in court.

**Why it matters:** many headlines called this a "life sentence". It was not a prison sentence at all. A hospital order is reviewed by doctors, and that is exactly what reopened the case two years later.

## What the hack cost Rockstar

The figure most often quoted comes from the December 2023 hearing: Rockstar told the court that recovering from the intrusion cost it $5 million, plus thousands of hours of staff time. That is Rockstar's own estimate as reported from court, not an audited number, and it covers the response to the break-in, not lost sales.

**Why it matters:** set against the scale of the project it is small. Our breakdown of [the GTA 6 budget](/blog/gta-6-budget/) shows why a seven-figure cost was a nuisance and not a threat to the game.

## The Trailer 1 leak in December 2023

The same month as the hospital order, GTA 6 leaked again, in a much smaller way. On December 4, 2023, a copy of Trailer 1 appeared on X shortly before its planned debut. Rockstar responded within the hour by publishing the trailer itself, about 15 hours ahead of schedule, and asked people to watch the real thing on YouTube.

No report we have read connects this to the 2022 intrusion or to Kurtaj, who was in custody at the time. The details of each official video are in [all GTA 6 trailers](/news/gta-6-trailers/).

**Tip:** when you see "the GTA 6 leak" mentioned, check which one is meant. The 2022 hack, the 2023 trailer and later incidents are separate events.

## What happened to the GTA 6 hacker in 2026

A routine assessment in 2025 found him fit to plead, and the CPS reopened the case. He was moved from hospital to prison in January 2026. In March 2026 he was found with a mobile phone in prison, which became one more charge.

On November 16, 2026, at Woolwich Crown Court, he pleaded guilty to three counts of unauthorised acts to impair the operation of a computer, one count of fraud, one count of unauthorised access with intent, and possessing a mobile phone in prison. ITV News also reported two counts of assaulting an emergency worker.

**Why it matters:** the 2023 jury had only decided that he did the acts. The 2026 pleas are the first convictions in the ordinary sense, which is why the CPS headline that day used the word "convicted".

## The GTA 6 hacker sentence

On November 23, 2026, four days after launch, Mr Justice Turner sentenced him to seven years. He left court the same day, released on licence, because the time he had already spent in hospital and prison covered most of the term. Reports of the hearing say the judge called him a very talented young man and told him not to let it happen again.

Release came with conditions. A five-year Serious Crime Prevention Order restricts his use of online services, including Telegram and Discord.

**Why it matters:** "released" does not mean "cleared". He is a convicted offender serving the rest of a seven-year sentence in the community, under an order that can send him back to court if he breaks it.

## Did the hack change GTA 6?

Not in any way Rockstar or Take-Two has acknowledged.

- **Development.** In November 2022, Take-Two chief executive Strauss Zelnick told investors the leak would not influence development and that there was no evidence significant assets had been taken.
- **The schedule.** Trailer 1 arrived in December 2023 with a 2025 window. The two later delays, to May 26, 2026 and then to November 19, 2026, were explained by the need for more polish. Rockstar never cited the hack. Full history: [GTA 6 delays](/blog/gta-6-delays/) and the [release date guide](/news/gta-6-release-date/).
- **The game.** The footage was from an early build. What shipped is covered in [GTA 6 gameplay](/news/gta-6-gameplay/).
- **Security.** Zelnick said the incident made the company more watchful about cybersecurity. Rockstar has not published details of what it changed.

**Why it matters:** the most visible effect was on fans, not on the game. Community projects such as the [GTA 6 mapping project](/blog/gta-6-mapping-project/) had to write rules about what material they would accept.

## Later breaches that were not the same hacker

Two 2026 incidents are often mixed up with this case.

In April 2026, the ShinyHunters group claimed to have data taken from Rockstar through a third-party analytics provider. Rockstar confirmed that a limited amount of non-material company information had been accessed and said there was no impact on the company or its players.

Later in 2026, an anonymous party using the name "Cyberleek" published footage ahead of launch. That is a separate matter from the 2022 case, and we do not cover its contents here.

## What is not confirmed

- **How much was taken beyond the clips.** Source code was threatened, and outlets differ on what else left the building. Take-Two's public position was that there was no evidence of significant assets being stolen.
- **Whether any ransom was paid.** Reports say Rockstar did not engage. Rockstar has not commented.
- **What Rockstar changed internally.** No official account exists.
- **How Trailer 1 got out early.** Rockstar has never said.
- **The exact licence terms** beyond the Serious Crime Prevention Order reported from court.

## How to tell real GTA 6 news from fake leaks

Now that the game is out, "leaks" have turned into bait: fake PC builds, fake online betas and fake DLC.

1. **Check Rockstar first.** New content is announced on the Rockstar Newswire and its official accounts. Our [GTA 6 news round-up](/blog/gta-6-news-today/) and [update tracker](/blog/gta-6-update/) follow those.
2. **Never download a "leaked build".** GTA 6 is not on PC yet, so any PC download is fake at best. See [GTA 6 on PC](/pc/gta-6-pc-release-date/).
3. **Ignore sign-up pages for GTA 6 Online access.** No online mode has been dated. See [GTA 6 online](/online/gta-6-online/).
4. **Turn on two-step verification** for your Rockstar account, and for the PlayStation or Xbox account it is linked to.
5. **Use a unique password,** and never type it into a page you reached from a video description or a direct message.
6. **Do not share stolen footage.** Take-Two has had it removed since 2022, and forums ban it.

## What this means for you

The story of the GTA 6 hacker is finished as a court case and never mattered much as a game story. It cost Rockstar money and spoiled the first look at the game, but it did not move the release date by any account the company has given, and nothing from 2022 tells you anything useful about the game you can play today.
